Who built this road?

Point at a road and hoobiltit names it, says which agency owns it, when it was built or last improved, and which project did the work — read live from the county, state and federal records that hold the answer. Turn on drive mode and it names every road as you go.

Coverage

How much it can tell you depends on where you are.

There is no national road-ownership dataset, so the app says which tier answered rather than pretending every road is equal. More jurisdictions get added as their records turn out to be genuinely queryable.

Everything

Maricopa County, Arizona

Owner, who maintains it, the project that built or improved it, the plat, the year the road was declared public, pavement condition, and the parcels alongside.

State records

Arizona · Delaware · Georgia · Iowa · Kentucky · Louisiana · Massachusetts · Michigan · Montana · New Hampshire · New Mexico · New York · North Carolina · Ohio · Pennsylvania · South Dakota · Texas · Vermont · Virginia

Name and owner, plus the construction or improvement year wherever the state publishes one.

Nationwide

Everywhere in the US

The street name from Census TIGER/Line, ownership if the road is on the National Highway System, and a build year if you are standing on a bridge.

Sharing

Send someone the answer.

Everything the app finds is yours to pass on — as a card, as a document, or as the letter that asks an agency for what no public record publishes.

What you can share

  • A card for any road — the name, the years, who maintains it
  • The full report as a text file: owner, dates, the project, the plat and the road declaration, and every source consulted
  • The public-records request the app drafts for you, straight to Mail
  • The roads you drove, as a spreadsheet

What the app never does

  • No account, no sign-in, no server of ours at all
  • Records nothing about you, and publishes nothing anywhere
  • A shared card or report carries no coordinate
  • The drive log exports road names, not a track of where you went

Every lookup goes straight from your phone to the county, state or federal service that answers it. There is nothing in between to keep a copy, which is why the app can say it collects nothing and mean it literally. The privacy policy is short for the same reason.
