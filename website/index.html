Coverage

How much it can tell you depends on where you are.

There is no national road-ownership dataset, so the app says which tier answered rather than pretending every road is equal. More jurisdictions get added as their records turn out to be genuinely queryable.

Everything

Maricopa County, Arizona

Owner, who maintains it, the project that built or improved it, the plat, the year the road was declared public, pavement condition, and the parcels alongside.

State records

Arizona · Pennsylvania · Louisiana

Name and owner, plus the construction or improvement year wherever the state publishes one.

Nationwide

Everywhere in the US

The street name from Census TIGER/Line, ownership if the road is on the National Highway System, and a build year if you are standing on a bridge.

Shared map

Rate a road — and see what everyone else found.

Rating a road keeps the report on your phone. One switch, off every time you file, also puts it on a map everyone can see. What that publishes is deliberately narrow, and it is the same short list every time.

What a shared report carries

  • Your condition rating, on the county's own five-point scale
  • The issue, from the county's own maintenance categories
  • The county's published rating for that road, where one exists
  • The road, its segment identifier, the jurisdiction and the maintaining agency — all public record already
  • Where, rounded to about 100 metres
  • The date, with no time of day

What it never carries

  • Your note — the only free text in the app, and it stays on your device
  • The exact coordinate, or the cross streets that would sharpen it again
  • The time of day
  • Your name, email, Apple ID, or any device identifier

Reading the map needs no account at all. Adding to it needs iCloud, and you can take yours back down later. Because a shared report has no free text in it, there is very little a stranger can put there that anyone would object to — and a marker several people flag stops being drawn. The map is Apple's CloudKit: there is no server of ours in between, and we keep no copy. The privacy policy spells all of it out.
